# Pause the holder during a live capture

This records the first single-frame implementation. The user subsequently requested three angles; the current sequence and dataset collection are described in `MULTIANGLE_CAPTURE_2026-10-19.md`.

The Vercel app now distinguishes rotating preview frames from the single stationary frame used for analysis. Once the existing empty-view state machine detects an occupied holder, the signed USB connector requests a local motor hold, waits 300 ms, discards the queued camera frame, reads one fresh frame, verifies that the controller's step counter has not changed, and resumes rotation. Upload and inference happen after rotation resumes. No photo button, manual upload, or multiple-angle collection is required.

The controller is the identified ESP32 at MAC `3c:8a:1f:09:88:8c`, connected through the CP210x adapter. Its firmware preserves the recovered pin order 18/21/19/22, 2048 steps per revolution, and 10 RPM. The candling light is not controlled by this firmware. The camera remains on its separate identified FTDI adapter. Both USB cables must remain connected to the laptop, and `scripts/upload_box_frames.py` must remain running.

`firmware/spinner/ControlledSpinner/ControlledSpinner.ino` accepts nonce-bound local serial STATUS, HOLD, and RUN commands. HOLD keeps the current coil phase and automatically expires within 12 seconds if the connector fails. `scripts/spinner_control.py` verifies the controller MAC, hold acknowledgement, and unchanged step counter; it sends RUN even if camera acquisition fails. A failed or expired hold discards the frame. The cloud has no general motor-control endpoint.

The device HMAC covers the upload body and its `usb-motor-preview-...` or `usb-motor-still-...` idempotency key. Browser uploads cannot claim this protocol. Preview uploads can request a stationary acquisition but cannot trigger inference themselves. A completed scan persists one `box-still-...` event and `captureProtocol: local-motor-hold-v1`; subsequent frames remain latched until the egg is removed. Repeated requests retain the existing durable, idempotent recovery behavior. Legacy browser and Render capture paths retain their previous behavior; this local pause integration targets the Vercel connector.

The new application image was built with ESP32 Arduino core 3.3.12, `esp32:esp32:esp32:FlashMode=dio,FlashFreq=40`. Its SHA-256 is `e819ff852907a45aa7f6108c03f79177c7ebeb48255eb57738fdeee3071acedf` (293200 bytes). The guarded installer, `scripts/flash_controlled_spinner.py`, checked controller identity and the previous application, saved the installed application, wrote only app0 at offset `0x10000`, and verified readback. Boot records, partition table, NVS, and camera firmware were preserved. Full backups and build receipts remain under ignored `deployment/private/`.

An eight-second bench hold reported an unchanged step counter and resumed stepping after RUN. The user's first short observation did not confirm a physical stop. During a second, extended hold, the user explicitly confirmed that the holder stopped and the light stayed on. A subsequent real camera acquisition held step 184095 unchanged, completed in 4.047 seconds, resumed at step 184098, and advanced to 184202 after 300 ms. A corrupt initial USB acquisition was discarded and the RUN cleanup executed; the fresh retry succeeded. Receipts are under `output/stationary-capture-20261018/`.

Commit `5a694b6` deployed successfully to the production Vercel app. The restarted Python connector's signed motor-preview frames were accepted, and the confirmed empty box reached `waiting` with `stationaryCapture: true` and no capture request. An automatic egg cycle still requires the user's observation after placement. The focused capture, USB, serverless, motor, Render, and production suites passed 94 tests. Tests cover failed camera acquisition, hold refusal, unexpected movement, preview behavior, and the server's separation of preview from analyzed frames.

This change does not establish exact incubation age or 99% accuracy. The existing experimental camera model and recapture gates remain unchanged. The first known Day 16 live example had Day 16 as its raw winner but did not meet the saved ambiguity margin; the app therefore requested another image.
