How the physical test was done

The operator identified each egg against the original incubation labels. Each attempt reopened the EMEET camera, discarded two warmup frames and analyzed three fresh views. At least two distinct accepted views had to agree, with no conflicting accepted class. Every attempted scan counts, including wrong grades, unclassified outcomes and acquisition failures.

Two sessions (D16-17 and D18-09) were acquired in the original physical trial. Their records are included once and preserved unchanged. The expanded protocol was set after those results were known. The remaining sessions use the same candidate and procedure. The eggs were reserved from weight fitting; they are the same previously examined cohort, not a new independent sample.

A monitor was visible behind the holder, and its displayed content changed during some scans. The egg remained visible in the reviewed views. We did not isolate the effect of the background, illumination or rotation angle. These local camera measurements do not test a deployed browser, automatic bin sorting, or a production site. Field accuracy has not been established.

Panel answers

1. How did you train the model?

We used ImageNet-pretrained MobileNetV2, trained the classifier head for three epochs, then fine-tuned the last five encoder blocks with AdamW. Only training images received augmentation. Class totals were balanced, and repeated views shared their egg’s training weight.

2. Why use this split?

We assigned whole eggs: 18 training, six validation and six reserved for testing. Training learned weights; validation selected the checkpoint and calibrated scanner thresholds. Keeping every frame of an egg in one partition prevents a second view of the same egg from masquerading as a new test subject.

3. Where are accuracy, precision, recall and F1?

See the first-scan and repeat-scan tables above. Reject has no physical test support and is marked “Not measured.”

4. How were the results computed?

Accuracy = correct / all attempted scans. Precision = TP/(TP+FP), recall = TP/(TP+FN), and F1 = 2TP/(2TP+FP+FN). An unclassified result or acquisition failure is incorrect and contributes an FN to the known class. For a represented class with no positive predictions, precision is reported as zero. Classes with no actual examples are not scored.

5. Did the same egg give a consistent result?

Each completed egg had ten fresh acquisitions. The per-egg table shows the observed counts and exact repeat-match result. A consistently wrong grade would still be an error.

6. Why can Day 16 and Day 18 be confused?

The labels are two days apart in recorded incubation time. The scanner observes candled appearance, not elapsed time. Developmental differences may be difficult to see under changes in shell, exposure or angle. The observed errors show overlap; this experiment does not establish a biological cause or a unique visual marker for either day.

7. Which epoch was selected, and why?

The selected original model used epoch 4. Training stopped after epoch 12, when eight epochs did not improve validation macro F1 beyond the specified threshold. The two later preprocessing variants did not beat the original validation result.

8. Was there data leakage?

Identified age eggs and exact image duplicates did not cross partitions in the original audit. Unknown public egg or batch relationships cannot be ruled out. Earlier results on these reserved eggs were known before the expanded physical test, so it is a controlled follow-up, not fresh independent validation.

9. Which CNN was tested?

The physical test used the locked original three-class MobileNetV2 on the local CPU. The camera supplied fresh images. The live website’s classifier was not replaced by this experiment.

10. What is the field-testing accuracy?

Field accuracy has not been established. The controlled physical result is 2/6 correct on the first scan per egg and 17/60 correct across repeated attempts. There were no physical Reject examples. These figures do not estimate performance across vendors, farms or batches.
